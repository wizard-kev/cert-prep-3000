Practice

Exam simulation

Paste a list of objectives: from an exam guide, a course outline, or your own notes. The app finds the matching objectives and asks questions on those only. Ignores the sections chosen above.

Question pool

How questions are checked

New questions are Unreviewed: they passed the app's quality checks. Once you answer one without reporting it, it becomes Passively-Validated. Choose Looks right after an answer to mark it Validated. Report a problem takes a question out of play at once (Disputed); you then confirm the report, which retires it, or undo it.

Export this certification as a pack

A pack is a .zip holding the certification's exam facts, objectives and questions: a copy for safekeeping or for another computer. Your progress isn't included. Packs built from a copyrighted study guide's content (the CCST objectives are) are for your own use: don't share them publicly.

      How the objective score works
      
Each answer scores 0 to 100 depending on whether it was right and how sure you were:

      
    
        
  • Right: 50 at 1 Guessing, rising to 100 at 5 Certain.
    • 
        
  • Wrong: 25 at 1 Guessing, falling to 0 at 5 Certain. Being sure and wrong costs the most.
    • 
      

      
An objective's score is the average of its answers, weighted toward recent ones: each older
        answer counts three quarters as much as the one after it. Status (Weak, Improving, Solid) is decided separately,
        by the rules in the tracker below.

    Getting started
    
    
      
      
    1. Pick the certification you're studying (top of the page). CCST Cybersecurity comes ready to use; set up others
        with Add a certification.
      2. 
      
    2. Tick the sections to study, then start Practice. Answer, rate how sure you were, and read the
        feedback.
      3. 
      
    3. Come back regularly. Practice brings back what you missed, and View progress shows how each
        objective is going and suggests where new questions would help.
      4. 
    
    

    
    Study modes
    
    
    
      
    Practice
    Feedback after every answer. Leans toward your weak objectives, brings back questions you
        missed (after 1, 3, then 7 days), and keeps introducing objectives you haven't tried.
    
      
    Focus
    Paste a list of objectives; the app matches it to the certification's objectives and asks
        questions on those only. See Focus.
    
      
    Exam simulation
    Timed, sized and weighted like the real exam (from the certification's exam facts),
        with no feedback until the end. Prefers questions you haven't seen. Mark questions to come back to; reports are
        held until the results page.
    
    
    

    
    Answering
    
    
    After choosing, rate how sure you are: 1 Guessing, 2 Unsure, 3 Leaning, 4 Confident, 5 Certain.
      There's no default, because honest ratings are what make progress tracking work: a right answer at 1 counts as a
      lucky guess, and a wrong answer at 4 or 5 keeps the objective Weak.
    
    
    Keyboard: letters A to F choose options, 1 to 5 set how sure you are, Enter submits or moves on.
      Recall first (home page) hides the options until you've worked out an answer, which makes you
      retrieve the idea rather than recognise it.
    
    
    After an answer: Looks right marks the question Validated; Report a problem (or
      Disagree with this answer? after a miss) takes it out of play; Ask Claude about this
      builds a prompt that explains it.
    

    
    Question statuses
    
    
    
      
    Unreviewed
    Passed the app's import checks; nobody has answered it yet.
    
      
    Passively-Validated
    You've answered it and didn't report a problem.
    
      
    Validated
    You marked it Looks right.
    
      
    Disputed
    You reported it. It's out of play until you confirm the report (it's retired) or undo it, on the
        results page or under Disputed questions on home.
    
      
    Retired
    Taken out for good: a confirmed report, or replaced by a better question. Answers to reported
        questions stop counting toward your progress; answers to replaced ones still count.
    
    
    

    
    Objective levels
    
    
    
      
    New
    Not practised yet.
    
      
    Weak
    Fewer than 60% of the last five answers right, or a recent wrong answer while Confident or Certain.
    
      
    Improving
    Getting there, but not yet Solid.
    
      
    Solid
    At least three right answers (not guesses) across two or more sessions and two or more angles, with
        the last five at 80% or better. Solid objectives come back for a check after 3, 7, then 14 days.
    
    
    

    
    Scores
    
    
    Session scores. Results show your score on all questions, on validated questions only (questions
      you'd answered or marked Looks right before the session), and on questions new to you versus repeats. Reported
      questions are left out. In an exam, unanswered questions count as wrong. Pass or fail is shown only when the
      certification publishes its passing score as a percentage.
    
    
    Objective score (0 to 100). Each answer scores by whether it was right and how sure you were: right
      is 50 at Guessing rising to 100 at Certain; wrong is 25 at Guessing falling to 0 at Certain. An objective's score
      averages its answers, with each older answer counting three quarters as much as the next, so recent answers matter
      most.
    

    
    Working with Claude
    
    
    The app runs no AI itself. Instead it writes prompts for you to paste into a chat at claude.ai, and imports what
      Claude sends back. Prompts carry the objectives, the rules the import enforces, and (when it helps) the wrong options
      you tend to pick, so new questions target your actual mix-ups.
    
    
      
      
    • Question prompt: choose objectives in the Claude workspace (or use a suggestion on the Progress
        page), build the prompt, paste it into Claude, then paste Claude's reply under Import.
      • 
      
    • Repair prompt: if some questions fail the checks, the app lists why and gives a prompt asking
        Claude to fix just those.
      • 
      
    • Study-session prompt: a tutoring conversation on the objectives you choose, using your progress.
      • 
      
    • Ask Claude about this: an explanation of one question, including your answer.
      • 
    
    

    
    Focus
    
    
    Paste up to 60 lines: objective names, topics, IDs such as C01-09, or lines copied from an official exam guide
      ("1.2 Explain common threats: malware, ransomware, DoS"). Each line is matched to the certification's objectives;
      topic lists are matched topic by topic. The preview shows every match with how sure the match is. Weak matches start
      unticked, and you can add any objective by searching. Lines that match nothing are reported as not in the
      objective list; objectives with no questions offer a prompt to write some.
    

    
    Certifications
    
    
    Add a certification walks you through four steps: name it; ask Claude for the official exam facts
      (time, number of questions, passing score, domains and weights, each marked official, estimate or unknown);
      paste the vendor's objective list so Claude turns it into objectives; then build the first questions in batches. You
      can stop at any step and continue later from home.
    
    
    Packs move a certification between computers or keep a copy: export one from the home page
      (Question pool), import one with Add a certification. A pack never includes progress, and importing
      never overwrites anything.
    

    
    Question bank health
    
    
    Shows whether every objective has at least two questions from two angles, and whether the right answers give
      themselves away across the pool (for example by usually being the longest option). Gaps come with a button that
      builds the prompt to fill them.
    

    
    Progress and backups
    
    
    Your sessions and answers are saved on this computer and survive restarts and updates. The app backs them up
      before every upgrade, reset and restore; Backups on home lets you make one yourself, restore one, or
      download a copy.
    

    
    Privacy
    
    
    Everything stays on this computer. The app makes no network connections, holds no passwords or API keys, and only
      answers requests from this computer. Prompts go to Claude only when you copy and paste them yourself.
    

    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    Progress backups 
    
    
    Backups hold your sessions and answers for every certification. The app makes one before every
      upgrade, reset and restore, and keeps the newest 10 of each kind in the data\backups folder.
    
    
    
      
      
    
    
    
    
    
    Restore replaces all progress with the backup's. Your current progress is backed up
      first, so you can undo a restore by restoring that copy. Question statuses (validated, reported, retired) stay as
      they are now. Download saves a copy elsewhere; to restore it later, put it back in data\backups
      under the same name.
